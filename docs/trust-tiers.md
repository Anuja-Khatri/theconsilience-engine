# Trust Tiers

## The rule: nothing unlabelled ships

Every published claim carries a tier label. The label describes the extraction quality, never the judge. The judge is always deterministic code regardless of tier.

## Tier A: Signed

Claims reviewed by a named expert. All paid work. All published case studies. The expert's name is attached to the approval. This is the highest trust level.

## Tier B: Machine-extracted

Claims that passed the cross-model agreement gate. Rendered with a visible badge and a banner stating that claims are machine-extracted and dual-model gated, while the verdict below is deterministic.

The badge is always visible. A reader can immediately see whether a claim was human-reviewed or machine-extracted.

## User-amended

A claim edited or dismissed by a user. The original is preserved. The comparison is relabelled. The amendment has an audit trail showing what changed and who changed it.

## Why this matters

In all three tiers, the verdict layer (matching, grading, testability) is identical and deterministic. The label describes how the CLAIM was extracted, not how the VERDICT was reached. The verdict is always code.

This is how "nothing ungraded ships" evolved into "nothing unlabelled ships" without touching the constitutional line that the judge is never a model.
