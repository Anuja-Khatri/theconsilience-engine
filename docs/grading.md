# Four-Rung Grading Ladder

## Why scores are dangerous

A number without a ceiling on what it does not license is noise. "Similarity: 0.87" tells you nothing about whether two theories structurally correspond, partially correspond, or merely share vocabulary. Scores invite misuse. Rungs with ceilings prevent it.

## The four rungs

### Rung 1: Structural identity (formal isomorphism)

Complete structural match. One theory's typed graph maps exactly onto a substructure of the other, with matching node types, matching edge types, and matching causal directions.

**Hard cap:** In cross-theory comparisons, Rung 1 is never assigned. Complete structural identity between rival theories is a probable extraction error, not a genuine finding. The highest achievable cross-theory grade is Rung 2 (structural analogy).

**Ceiling:** Does not license ontological identity. Two theories can be structurally isomorphic and still describe different things. The match is structural, not metaphysical.

### Rung 2: Structural analogy (partial mapping)

Substantial common subgraph. Most of the structure maps, with divergence points identified. The MCS ratio quantifies how much structure corresponds.

**Ceiling:** Does not license claims about the non-matching parts. The analogy holds where the structure matches. Where it does not match, the theories genuinely diverge, and the specific divergence points are named.

### Rung 3: Generative analogy

Thin overlap. Suggestive, not evidential. Some structural elements match but the correspondence is fragile, vocabulary-dependent, or pending resolution of an equivocation.

**Ceiling:** Does not license structural claims. May generate hypotheses worth testing. Must not be cited as evidence of correspondence.

### Rung 4: Thematic resonance (quarantined)

Vocabulary overlap only. The equivocation test found that the shared term was doing all the work and no structural correspondence survives without it. Quarantined: visible in the output but marked as not structural.

**Ceiling:** Does not license any structural claim. This is a vocabulary coincidence, not a correspondence. The specific reason it failed is published.

## Two-axis output

Every graded item carries BOTH a correspondence rung AND a demarcation label:
- **Falsifiable:** an experiment could settle it
- **Metaphysical:** no experiment could settle it (both theories deny the relevant observable)
- **Unsupported:** insufficient structural evidence for any rung
- **Contested:** the community disputes the interpretation

A framework can be honestly testable yet structurally empty (falsifiable, Rung 4). Or structurally tight yet untestable (Rung 1, metaphysical). Both axes matter.

## Machine-readable ceilings

Every ceiling is a structured statement, not prose. It is queryable, exportable, and attachable to any downstream use of the verdict. A system that consumes Consilience output can programmatically check what the correspondence does not license.
